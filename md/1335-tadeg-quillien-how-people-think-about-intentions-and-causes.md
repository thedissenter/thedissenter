RECORDED ON APRIL 2nd 2026.  
Dr. Tadeg Quillien is a chancellor’s fellow (research-focused faculty member) in the Psychology department at the University of Edinburgh. He is interested in the kinds of representations that the human mind is designed to spontaneously create. He adopts a ‘reverse-engineering’ approach to that question, using conceptual tools from evolutionary biology, statistics and computer science to ask what a mind that is well-designed (from the point of view of natural selection) should look like.

In this episode, we start by talking about how people think about intentionality. We discuss whether social stereotypes are “energy-saving devices” or “fit”. We talk about the study of causal inference, counterfactuals, and how people decide which causes to select. We discuss the link between altruism and commitment. Finally, we talk about factive mindreading.
